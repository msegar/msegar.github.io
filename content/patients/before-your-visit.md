## What to bring

- **A medicine list.** Include doses, blood thinners, aspirin, supplements, and over-the-counter products.
- **Your symptom notes.** When it happens, how long, what you were doing, and what it feels like.
- **Watch or phone ECGs** (see below).
- **Other records.** Past ECGs, echocardiograms, stress tests, and a sleep study if you have had one.
- **Questions.** Write them down so you do not forget.

## How to export an ECG from your watch

**Apple Watch**

1. Open the **Health** app on your iPhone.
2. Tap **Browse**, then **Heart**, then **Electrocardiograms (ECG)**.
3. Tap a recording.
4. Tap **Export a PDF for Your Doctor**.
5. Tap the share icon and save the PDF to Files, or choose how to send it. Do not email it to this website. Ask the office how they want to receive it.

For irregular rhythm notifications, you can also take a screenshot of the alert and of your heart rate data in the Health app.

**Kardia (AliveCor)**

1. Open the Kardia app and select the recording.
2. Use the share or email option to create a PDF.
3. Save the PDF to your phone or print it.

**Other watches (Samsung, Fitbit, Pixel, Garmin, Withings)**

Most have a similar option inside their health app to save an ECG as a PDF. If you cannot find it, take clear screenshots of the recordings and the date and time.

## Tips for better recordings

- Record an ECG **while you have symptoms**, not only when you feel normal.
- Sit still, rest your arms on a table, and keep the watch snug on your wrist.
- Record a few, and note the time and what you felt.

## What will happen at the visit

Expect an ECG, a review of your history, and a discussion of next steps. Depending on what we find, I may order a wearable monitor, an ultrasound of the heart, or blood tests.
